Know Your Scam

Types of Scams & Online Fraud

Understanding what type of scam you've experienced is the first step toward reporting it correctly and getting the right help.

❤️

Romance Scams

Fraudsters create fake profiles on dating apps or social media to build fake romantic relationships with victims — then extract money over weeks or months.

Learn More →
🐷

Pig Butchering Scams

A sophisticated long-con: scammers build trust over weeks before introducing a fake crypto investment platform. Victims are "fattened" before the scammer vanishes with all funds.

Learn More →
₿

Crypto & Investment Fraud

Fake trading platforms, fraudulent crypto exchanges, unlicensed brokers, and Ponzi schemes that steal deposits under the guise of legitimate investing.

Learn More →
🎣

Phishing & Smishing

Fraudulent emails and SMS messages designed to steal login credentials, banking details, or personal information through fake websites or links.

Learn More →
🏪

Fake Online Shops

Websites that look like legitimate e-commerce stores but collect payment without delivering any goods. Often disappear within days of launch.

Report Site →
👤

Identity Theft

Using stolen personal information to open accounts, make purchases, or commit fraud in the victim's name. Often follows a data breach or phishing attack.

Learn More →
💼

Business Email Compromise

Scammers impersonate executives or suppliers via email to trick employees into transferring funds or sharing sensitive financial information.

Learn More →
📞

Imposter Scams

Fraudsters pose as government agencies (IRS, HMRC, Social Security), tech support, or banks to trick victims into paying fake penalties or fees.

Learn More →
🔄

Recovery Scams

Secondary scams that target fraud victims — promising to recover their money for an upfront fee. These are almost always scams themselves.

Learn More →
Identify & Act

What to Do After Any Scam

01

Stop Contact

Immediately cease all communication with the scammer. Do not send any more money, even if threatened.

02

Document Everything

Screenshot all messages, profiles, transactions, and any websites involved before accounts are deleted.

03

Contact Your Bank

Notify your financial institution immediately — quick action can sometimes reverse transfers.

04

Report the Scam

File a formal report with your national fraud authority. Use our guides to find the right one.
