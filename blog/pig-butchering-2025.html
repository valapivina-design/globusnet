The Pig Butchering Scam Is Evolving — New Entry Points in 2025

Pig butchering operations are no longer limited to "wrong number" texts and dating apps. Criminal networks have adapted rapidly, using AI-generated identities, professional networking platforms, and fake employment opportunities as new vectors.

The Original Template — and Why It's Changing

The classic pig butchering entry was simple: a WhatsApp message from an unknown number, an apology for the "wrong number," and a quick transition to friendly conversation. For years, this worked reliably. But growing public awareness of this tactic has pushed criminal networks to diversify their approach.

In 2024 and into 2025, fraud investigators and law enforcement agencies globally have documented a significant shift in pig butchering entry points. Here's what's emerging.

New Entry Point 1: LinkedIn and Professional Networks

Perhaps the most significant new vector is LinkedIn. Pig butchering operators have begun creating detailed, convincing LinkedIn profiles — complete with fabricated employment histories, endorsements from fake connections, and professional headshots generated by AI. They reach out under the guise of networking, business opportunities, or professional advice.

This approach is effective because LinkedIn users are primed to trust professional connections. The conversation naturally shifts toward "investment opportunities" the contact has been using successfully — leading eventually to the familiar fake crypto platform.

New Entry Point 2: AI-Generated Identities

AI-generated profile photos and even AI-driven conversation management are increasingly being used by pig butchering operations. Tools that generate photorealistic fake faces eliminate the risk of reverse image searches — a key defense consumers previously relied on. Some operations are also reportedly using AI chatbots to manage initial conversations at scale, with human operators taking over when interest is established.

New Entry Point 3: Fake Job Offers

Fraudsters posing as recruiters contact victims with attractive remote job opportunities. The "onboarding process" involves setting up accounts on what appears to be a company trading or investment platform. The victim is told their job includes managing simulated trades — which are actually deposits into the scam platform. This approach is particularly insidious because victims believe they are working, not investing.

New Entry Point 4: Gaming and Interest Communities

Discord servers, Reddit communities, and gaming platforms are emerging as contact points. Scammers join communities around shared interests — cryptocurrency, investing, gaming, fitness — and build genuine-seeming community relationships before steering toward investment conversations.

New Entry Point 5: Fake Customer Service Contacts

Victims searching for support with existing crypto exchanges or financial services are being targeted through fake customer service accounts — particularly on Twitter/X and Telegram. These accounts offer "help" and gradually redirect users toward fraudulent platforms.

The Constant Remains: The Fake Platform

Despite all the variation in entry points, the core mechanics remain unchanged. Every pig butchering operation eventually directs victims to a fraudulent investment platform that shows fabricated gains, allows small early withdrawals to build trust, then blocks withdrawals with fake fee demands when the victim tries to access significant funds.

Protecting Yourself in 2025

  • Be skeptical of unsolicited professional contacts on LinkedIn who quickly mention investment returns
  • Never invest through a platform recommended by someone you've only met online
  • Verify any investment platform against your national financial regulator's registry
  • Use AI face detection tools (like facecheck.id) to verify profile photos beyond reverse image search
  • Any "fee" required to withdraw your own investment funds is always fraud — stop immediately
